# The guide's first batch — 56 pages, cross-checked (06/10/2026)

Batch 1 is every species on the approved list that is edible, or is a dangerous lookalike of one, and had no page
yet: 56 pages. With the ten sample pages the guide now has 66.

They were checked the same way as the sample pages. Every safety fact was read on First Nature (FN), Wild Food UK (WF),
Wikipedia (WP) and, where it covers the species, the Woodland Trust (WT); only the articles, never reader comments.
As agreed: the majority decides, a tie takes the cautious answer, and every disagreement is listed below. Every
safety fact on every page names at least two different websites; the content check refuses a page that does not.

## Four things for you to decide

1. **Livid Pinkgill — deadly or poisonous?** First Nature says "deadly poisonous" (at least one death). Wikipedia
   says poisonous, "generally not considered to be lethal", though it reports deaths. Wild Food UK has no page. A tie,
   so the page says **Deadly**. The approved list still says Poisonous; the scan and the guide show the worse of the two.
2. **Fatal Dapperling's cap size.** Only First Nature gives a size (2–3.5 cm). Wild Food UK's poison article lists it
   among the "small size dapperlings" without numbers. The page shows 2–3.5 cm and names both. No other trusted site
   gives a size.
3. **Sheathed Woodtuft.** All three sites call it edible, and all three warn that it is very easily mistaken for the
   deadly Funeral Bell. First Nature: "only for looking, not for cooking"; sometimes only a microscope tells them apart.
   The page says edible and leads with that warning. You could mark it "not edible" instead.
4. **"What is it growing on?" holds one answer per species.** The deadly Wrinkled Conecap grows on woodchip and
   compost. It is recorded as "ground", so someone who answers "wood" for woodchip would not see it in Identify. Two
   ways to close this: let a species hold two answers, or add "woodchip counts as Not sure" to the question's hint.

## Warnings the pages now carry

- **Edible, but some people react:** Honey Fungus, Shaggy Parasol (First Nature advises not eating it at all),
  Chicken of the Woods (avoid any growing on yew; never raw).
- **Cook well:** Orange Birch Bolete (15–20 minutes), Brown Birch Bolete.
- **Builds up metals:** The Prince (cadmium), Amethyst Deceiver (arsenic from polluted ground).
- **Same place, same season, deadly twin:** St George's Mushroom (Deadly Fibrecap, late spring, bruises red),
  Fairy Ring Champignon (deadly white funnels in the same rings from mid-summer), Velvet Shank (the Funeral Bell
  appears in mild winters; check there is no ring), Wood Mushroom (Deathcap and Destroying Angel in the same woods).
- **Smell is not enough:** the Livid Pinkgill can smell of flour, like St George's Mushroom (Wikipedia).
- **No taste test anywhere.** The nibble-and-spit test some books use for brittlegills is left out on purpose: the
  guide never asks anyone to put an unknown mushroom in their mouth.

## Where a website has no page

- First Nature has none for The Miller or the Pale Oyster.
- Wild Food UK has none for Angel's Wings, Fatal Dapperling, Frosty Funnel, Fool's Webcap, Ivory Funnel, Jack
  O'Lantern, Lilac Fibrecap, Livid Pinkgill or the Wrinkled Conecap. Where its other pages describe one of these, those
  pages are cited under their own names (for example its Chanterelle page for the Jack O'Lantern, and its "most
  poisonous UK fungi" article for the Fatal Dapperling and the Wrinkled Conecap).
- Wikipedia has no page of its own for the Lilac Fibrecap; it describes it as the lilac variety on its Inocybe
  geophylla page, cited under that title.
- First Nature treats the Ivory Funnel as the same species as the Fool's Funnel, so both pages cite that one page.

## A fix to the research tool

The tool that gathers the sources took a lookalike's page whenever the approved list cited one. The Funeral Bell had
been given Wild Food UK's Velvet Shank page. It now tries each species' own page first. Every gathered species was
checked: only the Funeral Bell, the Jack O'Lantern, the Lilac Fibrecap and the Fool's Webcap were affected. None of
their pages had been written yet, and all four were gathered again.
