# Core lists: what the source check changed (06/10/2026)

The starting lists were general knowledge. This check is what made them true. Every species and every pair below now
rests on two different websites. Sources used: First Nature (first-nature.com), Wild Food UK (wildfooduk.com), and,
where one of those had no page, Wikipedia. Everything here is in our own words. Only the article text of each page
counted, never the reader comments under it.

## How the rules were applied

- **Edible:** both pages say the species is eaten or edible. Many add "only when well cooked"; that goes on the
  species page later.
- **A dangerous lookalike (pair):** two websites each say the two species can be confused. The statement may be on
  either species' page. A warning about a whole group counts for each dangerous member of that group. For example,
  "small Lepiota species" counts for the Deadly Dapperling, and "the Amanitas" counts for the Deathcap. This only ever
  adds a warning, never removes one.
- **No dangerous lookalike:** both pages either say so, or name only harmless lookalikes. A page that names a
  dangerous group (for example "some Cortinarius species" or "other Russulas") rules this out.
- **Deadly or poisonous:** "deadly" when one source says deadly or potentially fatal outright and the other agrees the
  species is dangerous. Where the two sources disagree, the more dangerous answer is used, as the design says. A
  poisonous species is on the list only as a lookalike of an edible one; a deadly species always is.
- **Sources disagree on whether something is edible:** the cautious answer is used, so it is not listed as edible.

## Taken off the edible list

They will still be in the guide if they are among the most recorded UK species. They just aren't guaranteed a place
as "edible".

| Species | Why |
|---|---|

| Chlorophyllum rhacodes (Shaggy Parasol) | First Nature says it makes many people ill and should be avoided; Wild Food UK says it is edible but upsets some people. **Moved to the dangerous list** as a lookalike of the Parasol |
| Craterellus tubaeformis (Winter Chanterelle) | Wild Food UK names the Jelly Baby (possibly poisonous); Wikipedia records the Deadly Webcap being mistaken for it; no lookalike has two sources |

## Pairs dropped from the starting lists

These were the starting lists' guesses that two websites did not support:
- Field Mushroom, Wood Mushroom and Common Puffball with the Deathcap or Destroying Angel. Each was named by one site
  only. The Deathcap and Destroying Angel are in the list anyway, as deadly species.
- Giant Puffball with the Common Earthball.
- Penny Bun with the Devil's Bolete.
- Wood Blewit and Amethyst Deceiver with the White Fibrecap and the Lilac Bonnet.
- The Miller with the Livid Pinkgill.
- Honey Fungus with the Funeral Bell and the Sulphur Tuft (Honey Fungus itself left the edible list).

## Levels set by the cautious rule

- **Deadly:** Panthercap (First Nature says deadly, Wild Food UK says poisonous), White Fibrecap (the same), and
  Fool's Conecap (Wikipedia says deadly; First Nature is not sure whether it is even poisonous).
- **Poisonous:** False Chanterelle (Wild Food UK says poisonous; First Nature reports hallucinations and advises
  treating it as inedible). Livid Pinkgill is also poisonous: both sources mention reported deaths, but neither calls
  it deadly outright.

## Not added, for Stefan to decide

- **Clitocybe phyllophila (Frosty Funnel, iNaturalist: Collybia phyllophila):** First Nature calls it deadly. No second
  source was found: Wild Food UK has no page and Wikipedia has no article. It is left off the safety list until a
  second source is found. It can be added to the guide by hand.
- **Amanita gemmata (Jewelled Amanita):** Wild Food UK says it is "said to be deadly", while First Nature and Wikipedia
  say poisonous with deaths extremely rare. It is not anyone's lookalike in these sources, so it is not added.
