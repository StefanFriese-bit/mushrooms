# Cross-check of the safety list (06/10/2026)

Stefan, 06/10/2026: *"once the list has been populated, we would want to cross-reference it against … two or three
other large online sources of information to determine which items or which data is accurate."*

This report covers the safety list: every species the guide marks as edible, deadly or a dangerous lookalike. Each one
already rested on two websites (`reports/core-lists-research.md`). Here, each verdict was checked again against more
large sources, and the species the two-source rule had dropped were looked at again.

| Source | Used for | Coverage |

| ~~MushroomExpert~~ | Not usable | Every page says: "This site contains no information about the edibility or toxicity of mushrooms" |
| ~~mushroom.world~~ | Not usable | The same notice |
| ~~123pilzsuche.de~~ | Not usable | A large German site, but its pages mix text from different species, so a script cannot read them reliably |

So each species now rests on three or four sources: First Nature, Wild Food UK, Wikipedia, and for some the Woodland
Trust.

## How the votes were counted

- **Only what a source states counts.** A page that says nothing about a lookalike is not a vote for "no lookalike".
- **The majority decides. A tie takes the cautious answer.**
- **Wikipedia's text counts as well as its one-word fact box.** The box says "poisonous" for the Deadly Dapperling,
  while the article describes several deaths.
- **Warnings about a group count for pairs**, as before. For example, "other Russulas" counts for the Sickener. Such a
  warning can only add a lookalike warning, never remove one.

## Verdicts confirmed

- **All 33 edible species:** Wikipedia says "edible" or "choice" for 30, and the Woodland Trust agrees wherever it has
  a page. No source called any of them poisonous. Wikipedia gives no verdict for three: Terracotta Hedgehog, Orange
  Birch Bolete, and the Blusher, which has no article.
- **Deadly, confirmed by the majority, counting Wikipedia's text:** Deathcap, Destroying Angel, Funeral Bell, both
  dapperlings, Deadly Webcap, Fool's Webcap, Deadly Fibrecap, Lilac Fibrecap (2 to 1), Fool's Funnel, Ivory Funnel,
  False Morel, Brown Rollrim, Wrinkled Conecap and Angel's Wings.
- **Poisonous, confirmed:** Yellow Stainer, Livid Pinkgill, Jack O'Lantern, False Chanterelle, Common Earthball, Magpie
  Inkcap, Woolly Milkcap and Stinking Dapperling. For the Stinking Dapperling, Wikipedia says "possibly poisonous,
  regarded as suspect".

## Split decisions, left on the cautious side

- **Grey Knight:** Wild Food UK says deadly, First Nature says slightly poisonous (toxins that can cause fatal kidney
  damage), and Wikipedia says edible (the poisoning claim was disputed in 2016). Two of the three call it dangerous, so
  it stays on the list; deadly against poisonous is a tie, so the cautious answer, deadly, stands.
- **Frosty Funnel:** First Nature says deadly and Wikipedia says poisonous. It is a tie, so deadly stands.

## Still outside the safety list, though in the guide as "most recorded"

Sulphur Tuft, Common Inkcap, Lilac Bonnet, Wood Blewit, Field Blewit, Deceiver and Larch Bolete. For each, a lookalike
or a verdict rests on one source only. Their own pages, in stage 3, will be cross-checked the same way.
